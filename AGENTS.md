# Repository Guidelines

## Project Structure & Module Organization
This Maven project keeps code under `src/main/kotlin/rayTraceTypescript`, split into domain-focused packages such as `materials`, `objects`, and `utils`. The entry point is `Main.kt`, which wires up the `Camera`, scene configuration, and invokes `render`, producing `image.ppm` in the repo root. Build artifacts land in `target/`, and `pom.xml` pins Kotlin 2.2.21 plus the Exec plugin that targets `rayTraceTypescript.MainKt`. Keep any future assets (sample renders, textures) in a dedicated top-level folder so they do not pollute `target/`.

## Rendering Backends
Ray tracing runs on NVIDIA GPUs through CUDA (LWJGL's `lwjgl-cuda`: the driver API from `nvcuda.dll`, which the driver installs, and NVRTC from `native/cuda/windows-x64`, see its README - no CUDA Toolkit needed). The kernel is CUDA C++ in `src/main/resources/kernels/raytracer.cu`, compiled at run time by `gpu/CudaContext` for the card found with the scene's features switched in. `Camera.initialize()` produces the shared `CameraSetup`, `gpu/SceneFlattener` lays the scene out as flat buffers, and the kernel does sampling, materials and textures; the CPU only writes the PPM through `render/PpmWriter`. There is no CPU fallback: without a CUDA device a render fails with the reason.

Two engines find the hits, behind the `gpu/GpuSession` interface. `gpu/CudaSession` runs the kernel as it is, with its own BVH traversal (sm_61 on a GTX 1070). `gpu/optix/OptixSession` builds the same file with `-DRT_OPTIX=1` as an OptiX program: OptiX builds and searches the tree - on an RTX card its RT cores do - and only `world_hit` and the entry point differ, the path loop, materials, textures and light sampling being shared text. OptiX has no binding here: `gpu/optix/OptixApi` takes the table of functions the driver's `nvoptix.dll` hands out and calls them through libffi (`utils/NativeFunction`), and `gpu/optix/OptixLayout` asks the compiler how OptiX's structures are laid out, with a probe kernel built against the SDK headers - which are NVIDIA's and fetched, not committed (`native/optix/README.md`; OptiX 9.0, ABI 105, a driver of the 570 series or newer). On an RTX 2060 OptiX traces Yennefer 3.2 times as fast as the CUDA kernel (256 spp: 2.06 s vs 6.53 s); on a GTX 1070, which has no RT cores, it is the slower of the two (17.1 s vs 11.75 s). It takes every scene the CUDA kernel does. Spheres are custom primitives: a tree of their own over their boxes (a moving sphere's covering its whole path, an inside-out one's by the size of its radius), whose intersection program calls the kernel's `sphere_t`; the ray's time rides in a seventh payload value only in a scene with moving spheres (`-DOPTIX_RAY_TIME`), since declared everywhere it slowed Yennefer by 4%. Fog is tested after OptiX's search: `world_hit` runs the kernel's `medium_t` over each volume up to the nearest surface. On the RTX 2060 at 1080 wide and 256 spp, OptiX against the CUDA kernel: `model` with Yennefer on its sphere floor 1.02 s vs 3.10 s, `final-week` 2.10 vs 4.60, `cornell-smoke` 1.90 vs 3.52, `perlin-spheres` 0.60 vs 0.62. Rays end at 1e16 rather than infinity, which OptiX's validation rejects. `-Drt.optix.include` / `OPTIX_INCLUDE` and `-Drt.optix.dll` / `OPTIX_DLL` point at other headers and another library, `-Drt.optix.log=1..4` sets how much OptiX reports, `-Drt.optix.validate` has it check every call, `-Drt.optix.ptx` feeds it PTX instead of OptiX IR.

`-Drt.gpus` picks the cards: `all` (the default), `one` (the best), or a list of numbers or pieces of names such as `2060` or `0,1`, each optionally with its engine (`2060:optix,1070:cuda`; naming a card twice runs it twice, for tests). `RT_GPU_DEVICE=<substring>` still names one card (`RT_OPENCL_DEVICE` is still read). `-Drt.backend` is `auto` (the default), `optix` or `cuda`: `auto` is OptiX on the cards with RT cores, where the headers and the library are there, and the CUDA kernel elsewhere; `gpu/SwitchingSession` takes such a card to the CUDA kernel for a scene OptiX does not trace (now only one of nothing but fog, which leaves OptiX no tree). Several cards render one image together through `gpu/MultiGpuSession`: they share its samples, not its pixels - the random streams are keyed by pixel and sample - each card on a thread of its own, into an output of its own, and the image is the sum. A card takes its share of the samples left by its speed, measured as it traces, and none of a launch too short for it, so a slow card never makes a launch longer; a card still loading a scene or building a kernel joins when it is ready, and the card doing most of the work traces on the caller's own thread. With the GTX 1070 on the CUDA kernel beside the RTX 2060 on OptiX, Yennefer at 256 spp takes 1.91 s (2.11 s on the RTX 2060 alone); in the viewer the second card adds about 6% at 540x960 and nothing at 1080x1920, where its one sample outlasts a launch. `-Drt.gpus.log` prints every two seconds what each card traced. In the viewer the list *Karty* in the panel's *Obraz* section switches cards while it runs, and the line under it names the cards and engines in use. A GTX 10 card and an RTX card together need a driver no newer than 581.57: the 590 series dropped the GTX 10 cards, and the security updates for them (582.xx) leave the RTX ones out.

`-Drt.nvrtc=<dir>` / `NVRTC_DIR` point at another NVRTC 12.x (e.g. a toolkit's `bin`), `-Drt.cuda.options`, `-Drt.cuda.log`, `-Drt.cuda.dump=<file>` and `-Drt.cuda.ptx` are for compiler experiments. The kernel began as a port of the OpenCL kernel that preceded it (last in commit `1b65ee9`); on a GTX 1070 it traces about 15% slower than that one did (Yennefer at 256 spp: 11.7 s of GPU time vs 10.1 s) with near-identical machine code, and Nsight Compute does not support Pascal, so the cause is unknown. The CPU path tracer that preceded both is gone too; its renders of the comparison scenes are kept as test references (`src/test/resources/cpu-reference`, `reference-image*.ppm.gz`). The scene classes keep their CPU `hit` code, which the viewer's picking and placement use. Because the GPU draws its random numbers per pixel and sample, renders match those references in geometry, shading and brightness but carry a different noise pattern - compare images statistically (as `GpuCpuComparisonTest` does), never byte for byte. Instances: the CPU classes transform rays at traversal time, the flattener folds the same rigid transform into the primitives.

## Meshes and 3D File Formats
`objects/MeshLoader` reads a model file into a `Mesh` - a flat triangle soup - picking a parser in `objects/formats` by extension: STL (binary and ASCII, told apart by file size rather than the `solid` keyword), OBJ, PLY (ASCII and both binary byte orders), OFF, glTF 2.0 (`.gltf` with external or data-URI buffers, and packed `.glb`), and 3MF. Only geometry is read; materials come from the scene. Faces with more than three corners are fanned into triangles, glTF node transforms are baked into world space, and glTF needs a JSON reader that `utils/Json` provides, because the project has no JSON dependency. `Mesh.toHittables` turns each facet into a `Triangle` - Moller-Trumbore intersection, barycentric u/v, and a normal taken from the winding rather than from the file, since mesh files frequently carry wrong ones. A model from a file is shaded smooth (`Mesh.smooth`, set by `MeshLoader`; `-Drt.smooth=off` keeps the facets): `Mesh.smoothNormals` welds corners that are bit for bit the same point (-0 taken as +0) and gives each the area-weighted sum of its facets' normals, as StlViewer does; the kernel blends a facet's three corner normals by the hit's barycentric coordinates (`smooth_normal`), takes the blend only when it leans the same way as the facet, and keeps the facet's own normal (`gnormal`) for what is in front and behind - a reflection or a bounce under the true surface ends the path. The CUDA layout carries the normals turned and in leaf order (`SceneBuffers.triangleNormals`, `MeshBlock.cornerNormals`), the OptiX one in each mesh's own space, turned per hit; `HAS_SMOOTH` keeps the code out of scenes without a smooth mesh. Meshes made in code, such as the tests' octahedrons, stay flat. Every sample is also capped at `Camera.fireflyClamp` (its brightest channel; 16, StlViewer's cap, `-Drt.firefly=<n>|off`; studio-model, whose exposure is in its lamps, uses 16 times that exposure); the tests against the stored CPU renders keep every sample.

Model files agree on no units or origin, so `standingOnFloor()` is the default placement: the model is centred over the origin in x/z, rests on y=0 - the top of the ground sphere every scene uses - and is scaled to `DEFAULT_TARGET_HEIGHT`; pass `targetHeight = null` to keep the authored size. `BookScenes.modelWorld(path, material)` assembles that default scene (checkered ground + model) and always wraps it in a `BvhNode`. Run it with `mvn exec:java -Drt.scene=model -Drt.model=<path>`; scenes live in `scenes/BookScenes` and are picked by name (the old `1|2|3` still work), and the path also comes from `MODEL_FILE` or `./model.stl`. Triangles get their own leaf kind in the GPU buffers (`SceneBuffers.LEAF_TRIANGLE`) and their bounding boxes are padded through `Aabb.padded()`, since a facet in an axis-aligned plane would otherwise have a box no ray can enter.

## Scenes and Sampling
Every scene from the three books lives in `scenes/BookScenes` as a named builder returning a
`SceneDefinition`; `-Drt.scene=<name>` picks one (`cornell-final`, `final-week`, `cornell-smoke`,
`perlin-spheres`, `quads`, …, and the old `1|2|3` still resolve). `-Drt.samples`, `-Drt.width`,
`-Drt.depth` and `-Drt.aspect` scale and reframe any scene from the command line, since the books quote their finales at sample
counts that take hours, and `-Drt.out` redirects the render away from the committed `image.ppm`.

Shading follows The Rest of Your Life. A material returns a `ScatterRecord`: a diffuse surface
hands back a density to sample (`CosinePdf` around the normal, `SpherePdf` inside fog) while a
mirror or glass hands back the single ray it sends, and the renderer divides each sample by the
density it came from. A scene may also name `Camera.lights` - shapes worth aiming at - and then
half of every diffuse bounce's samples go straight at a light (`MixturePdf` over `HittablePdf`
and the surface's own density), which is what clears the noise out of a dim room; `-Drt.lights=off`
renders the same scene without it. `Quad` and `Sphere` implement `pdfValue`/`random` for this, and
the GPU carries the light list as bare geometry separate from the tree it traverses. Emissive
surfaces only emit through their front face.

## Build, Test, and Development Commands
- `mvn compile` — compiles Kotlin sources with the Kotlin Maven plugin targeting JVM 17.
- `mvn clean package` — recreates the shaded jar under `target/` and is the baseline for CI.
- `mvn exec:java` — runs `MainKt`, generating/overwriting `image.ppm`; commit only curated outputs.
- `mvn test` or `mvn -Dtest=CameraTest test` — executes unit tests (add them under `src/test/kotlin`).
- `GpuCpuComparisonTest` and `ImageRegressionTest` need a CUDA device and skip themselves without one. They compare against CPU renders stored before the CPU path tracer was removed; those references are frozen and cannot be rendered again, so a scene added to the comparison needs a reference made some other way. `UPDATE_REFERENCE_IMAGE` no longer exists.

## Coding Style & Naming Conventions
Follow idiomatic Kotlin style: 4-space indentation, `UpperCamelCase` for classes such as `HittableList`, `lowerCamelCase` for functions/properties, and `SCREAMING_SNAKE_CASE` only for constants. Keep packages under `rayTraceTypescript.*` to mirror directories. Favor `val` over `var`, keep mutable lists encapsulated (as in `HittableList`), and document non-obvious math near vector or material logic. Reuse helper extensions (e.g., `Vector.minus`) instead of ad-hoc calculations to preserve consistency.

## Testing Guidelines
Add tests beside the mirrored package in `src/test/kotlin`, using Kotlin Test or JUnit 5 so Maven Surefire can discover them. Name test classes `<Class>Test` and methods `fun rendersBlurredBackground()` so failures read naturally. Test deterministic pieces (vector math, material scatter logic, random helpers with seeded RNG) and gate image regressions by comparing computed pixel statistics rather than raw ppm bytes. Run `mvn test` locally before pushing; target coverage that hits every material branch at least once.

## Commit & Pull Request Guidelines
Git history shows short imperative messages (`add .gitignore`, `finished ray tracing in one weekend`), so keep titles under ~72 characters, starting with a verb (`tune camera depth`). Each PR should describe the scene/camera changes, list commands run (e.g., `mvn clean package`), attach before/after crops of `image.ppm` when visuals change, and link tracking issues. Avoid bundling refactors with feature additions; open follow-up tickets for larger rewrites.

## Rendering Output & Assets
Generated `image.ppm` files can be large; ignore intermediate renders and only commit canonical samples referenced in docs. When tweaking `camera` defaults (aspect ratio, samples, depth), capture the rationale in PR notes so reviewers understand performance vs. fidelity trade-offs. Store environment-specific configs (e.g., higher sample settings) in local profiles rather than changing `Main.kt` defaults.

Every render writes a PNG beside its PPM (`render/PpmWriter`, same pixels); `-Drt.pfm` adds the unclipped linear pixels as a PFM. Renders started from `Main` are denoised by default (`-Drt.denoise=off` disables it): beside the raw image they write `<name>-denoised.ppm/.png`: after the image, the kernel renders the two guides, first-hit albedo and normal, in one pass (`GpuSession.MODE_GUIDES`: `AOV_MODE 1` with `GUIDES`, which writes the normal into an output of its own; 256 spp) and a denoiser behind `render/ImageDenoiser` cleans it. By default (`-Drt.denoiser=auto`) that is NVIDIA's OptiX denoiser (`gpu/optix/OptixDenoiser`, the AOV model, normals in world space, its weights come with the driver) on a card that does not trace best - the GTX 1070 beside the RTX 2060, which `-Drt.gpus=all` then leaves out of the tracing - or on the only card; `-Drt.denoiser.gpu=<number or name piece>` picks the card, `-Drt.denoiser=oidn` goes back to Intel Open Image Denoise's RT filter on the CPU (`render/Denoiser`, its DLL called through LWJGL's libffi), which is also what runs when OptiX is not to be had. On a Yennefer frame at 1080x1920 and 64 spp, OIDN on the four-core i5 takes 1.34 s, OptiX on the GTX 1070 0.31 s (0.26 s of it the denoiser, the rest the PCIe x4 copies) and on the RTX 2060 0.07 s; against a 4096-spp render OptiX scores 34.0 dB PSNR and OIDN 33.3 dB, and the two cards give the same image. Denoised stills and films trace at half the width and height by default (`-Drt.upscale=2`; `-Drt.upscale=off` renders at full size) (`CameraSetup.halved`: each pixel at the middle of a 2x2 block) and has OptiX's UPSCALE2X model denoise and enlarge in one call (`ImageDenoiser.upscale`), (the raw still is written at half size, the denoised one at full; the viewer ignores it). That model needs a Volta or newer card: on the GTX 1070 it returns a smeared image with ghost edges and no error (Cornell box 33.7 dB against 35.1 for bicubic enlarging, on the RTX 2060 40.5 dB), so it runs on the RTX 2060 (`GpuRenderer.canUpscale`), and a film then traces on that card alone - the GTX 1070 would cost more in per-frame scene copies than it traces. Yennefer at 64 spp: 0.19 s a film frame instead of 0.50 (trace 140 ms, guides 22, denoising 71 beside it), at 29.1-29.2 dB from the full-size render against 35.5 dB for full size vs a 4096-spp still - the gold's finest reflections, the face and the lacing, go soft. `-Drt.denoise.repeat=N` denoises N times more, to time a denoiser once it is set up. The OIDN DLLs ship in `native/oidn/windows-x64` (see its README), so a clone denoises as is; `-Drt.oidn` or `OIDN_DIR` point elsewhere. Missing DLLs only skip the denoising, with a message. `-Drt.aov=albedo|normal` renders a guide on its own. Gathering the guides in the image's own pass, from its camera rays, was tried (2026-11-16) and dropped: on the RTX 2060 through OptiX it slowed the image by 81 ms a film frame (407 to 488 ms), more than the separate passes took (87 ms), even with nothing gathered (+28 ms) or the sums written straight to memory (+72 ms). Reads come back through page-locked host memory (`DeviceMemory.allocateHost`): 2 ms for a 1080x1920 image instead of 4.

## Turntable Films
A turntable film turns the scene's objects in place while the camera, the lights and the room stand still - what StlViewer's "Film obrotowy" looks like (it turns its camera with the lamps and wall instead). `-Drt.turntable=film.mp4` renders one from the command line (`gpu/TurntableRender`: the parts that are neither lights nor walls, or `-Drt.turntable.parts=a;b`, about the middle of their foot - the footprint of their lowest 5% of height (`gpu/Footprint`), as StlViewer turns a figure, so a raised sword or an outstretched arm does not pull the axis aside and set the base wandering; the viewer's film turns its objects the same way; `-Drt.turntable.seconds|turns|fps|reverse|quality=max`, `-Drt.samples`); the viewer's panel button *Film obrotowy…* opens `gui/TurntableDialog` (the selected object, else all added ones, else the scene's own), and `-Drt.gui.film=film.mp4` starts that path without clicks. `gpu/TurntableFilm` traces each frame in ~100 ms slices with its own seed and 32-sample guides from one pass, and denoises and writes it on a worker while the GPU traces the next - with the default denoiser on the second card, the RTX 2060 traces Yennefer's frames back to back at 0.50 s a frame (trace 405 ms, guides 70 ms) (1.51 s when OIDN on the CPU set the pace), and a film ends by printing where a frame's time went; `-Drt.temporal=on` denoises a film whose turning parts are all placed meshes with OptiX's temporal model (TEMPORAL_UPSCALE2X, or TEMPORAL_AOV at full size), each frame carried into the next along a flow guide the guides pass traces (`PlacedMesh.turning`, `FrameTurn`: a hit on a turning mesh turned back a frame and projected onto the still camera; 0.006 px from the CPU's on average), with the history primed on the turn's last 8 frames so the loop does not jump - off by default, since on the gold Yennefer in fog it halved the background's flicker at 16 spp but cost the figure 1 dB against 512 spp, and 2.3 dB at 64 spp: gold's highlights come from the still lamps and smear along the surface's flow (a trust map keeping the flow off metal saved the figure and made the background flicker more); `render/VideoEncoder` pipes the frames to ffmpeg (`-Drt.ffmpeg`, `FFMPEG` or the PATH; H.264 High, yuv420p, BT.709, key frame every 2 s, StlViewer's bitrates), renders one turn and copies it for the others, and writes `<name>.part.mp4` until the film is whole, so a cancelled or failed film leaves nothing. `gpu/Placement` folds a move of a `PlacedMesh` into its placement, so turning one costs a tree copy per frame, not a rebuild; `studio-model` (StlViewer's gold-reel studio, for comparing the two renderers) builds its figure that way.

## Interactive Viewer
`-Drt.gui` opens the chosen scene in a window (`gui/Viewer`) instead of rendering to a file: left drag orbits around the point the camera looks at, Shift + left drag or middle drag pans, the wheel or right drag dollies, Ctrl + click stands the selected model on the floor under the cursor, R resets, D toggles denoising, S asks for a file name (suggested `viewer-<scene>-<time>` beside `-Drt.out`) and saves PPM/PNG plus the denoised copy, Ctrl+S / Ctrl+O save and load the whole scene as a `.rtscene` text file (`gui/SceneFile`: base scene and its switches, camera, image settings, mode, lights and parts, each added object with its model path relative to the file; `-Drt.gui.load=file` opens one from the command line, picking its base scene), +/- change the field of view, Delete removes the selected model. The scene's own objects (`BuiltInObject`) move and turn like added ones - both are `Movable` - by standing them in `Translate(RotateY(...))` about their footprint and moving their light-sampling stand-in along; "Przywróć obiekty sceny" puts them back. Parts marked `isSurface` (the Cornell box walls) are `WallObject`s instead: fixed in place, their material editable (a changed wall is rebuilt as the same quad); added objects and walls share `HasMaterial`, and scene files keep changed walls as `wall.N.*`. The side panel (`gui/ControlPanel`) sets the resolution and the sample target and adds models from any format `MeshLoader` reads (`gui/PlacedObject`: position, turn, height, finish, z-up for 3D-printing files); `-Drt.gui.models=a.stl;b.obj` opens with models placed. A mesh may carry per-face materials (`Mesh.faceMaterials` into `Mesh.palette` of `MeshMaterial`, read from an OBJ's `mtllib`/`usemtl` and .mtl `Kd`, `Pm`, `Pr`, `Ke`, `d`, `Ni`); transforms keep them, `MeshBlock` carries them in leaf order, `PlacedMesh.palette` and `toColouredHittables` use them, and `cornell-model` keeps them unless `-Drt.model.material` is given (it also stands only STL/3MF up from z). It opens in the quick look for setting a scene up (kernel `AOV_MODE 3`: first hit shaded by its colour under a head light, glass seen through, 16 jittered rays per pixel at full size); T or the panel's *Tryb* switches to ray tracing, and `-Drt.gui.raytrace` opens in it. Object edits rebuild the GPU scene 80 ms after the last change, in the same `GpuSession` (`replaceScene` swaps the scene buffers and keeps the kernels while the feature switches match). A loaded model keeps a `gpu/MeshBlock` - its triangles and SAH tree built once in its own space - and is placed as a `PlacedMesh` (scale, turn about y, offset): the flattener copies that tree in transformed and grafts it into the scene's tree as one primitive, so moving, turning or resizing a 400k-triangle model costs ~130 ms instead of a ~2 s rebuild. In ray tracing, while the camera moves it renders as a turntable film does at its quickest: half size, 16 spp, guides with the flow the camera's move makes (`setCamera(setup, previous)`: the camera of the frame before rides in the camera buffer, so both engines trace it), enlarged by OptiX's temporal denoiser (`GpuRenderer.motionDenoiser`, TEMPORAL_UPSCALE2X on the RTX 2060) that carries each frame into the next, and denoised beside the tracing of the next frame - Yennefer at 1080x1920 in 10-12 frames a second, the RTX 2060 doing both (trace 20-35 ms, guides ~50, denoising ~75 as they share the card); without OptiX or with `-Drt.gui.motion=plain` it keeps the quarter-size 1-spp preview. `-Drt.gui.spin=<degrees a second>` keeps the camera orbiting to time that, and the log prints frames a second every 2 s. Once it holds still for 150 ms it accumulates full-size samples, denoising at 16, 64, 256… spp. The look is FlatLaf's dark theme (`com.formdev:flatlaf`, set up in `gui/Theme` before any component is made) with an amber accent; shortcuts go through a window-wide key dispatcher, so they work whatever holds the focus except text fields. It shares `gpu/GpuSession` with the file renderer: scene buffers uploaded once, one kernel per mode, the camera and resolution passed per launch, and output buffers for the denoiser guides.
